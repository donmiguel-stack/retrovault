**2. What I plan to sell**

Retro Vault is a free, offline game library for machines from 1978–1995: the Philips Videopac
G7000/G7400 (Magnavox Odyssey² in the US), the Commodore 64, the Commodore Amiga and MS-DOS.
Around 400 releases are catalogued with box art, manuals, history and gameplay clips, and the
emulators are built in so out-of-print titles play straight in the browser. That part is free and
stays free.

The shop exists for one narrow purpose. People still write **new** games for these machines today
— homebrew, made by one or two hobbyists over months — and they have almost nowhere to sell them.
What I would sell through Lemon Squeezy is exactly that:

- **The products**: new homebrew games as digital downloads. Small files — a Videopac cartridge
  image is 2–16 KB; an Amiga or MS-DOS title a few megabytes.
- **How they are made and licensed**: written by the individual developer, who keeps the copyright.
  Each title is listed only with that author's **written permission**, non-exclusive, and they can
  pull it at any time. The revenue is theirs; Retro Vault keeps a small share to cover payment
  fees, hosting and the licensing server. Nothing is listed without an agreement in writing first.
- **Who they are for**: retro-gaming enthusiasts and collectors, mostly in Europe and North America.
- **Single purchases, not subscriptions.** One payment, one game, licence that never expires. No
  recurring billing of any kind.
- **How a purchase is fulfilled**: Lemon Squeezy issues a licence key for the order and emails it.
  The buyer opens the game's page in the Vault, clicks "I have a licence key" and pastes it. Our
  server (retrovault.world/store/api.php) validates the key against your Licence API and then
  serves the game file, so the file is never a public URL. The game starts in the browser, and the
  buyer can also download their copy to keep and run offline or on real hardware. Each copy carries
  an invisible code tied to the order, so a copy that turns up on a ROM site can be traced. A key
  activates on up to 10 of the buyer's own devices, and they can free a slot themselves.

For completeness: the library also hosts free homebrew and public-domain titles as plain downloads.
Those are not sold and never will be.
